# 10. Pitch and communication plan

## Narrative

**From orbital data to an understandable exploration plan.** Mars data exists across missions and archives. Apex-Mars brings a focused Jezero case study into one interface while exposing sources and assumptions.

Do not promise certified navigation, a validated suit simulator, live weather or completed features that only appear in the roadmap.

## Three-minute English pitch

| Time | Message |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:25 | “A destination is only one part of a Marswalk. What matters is what we can learn, what constraints we face and how we return.” |
| 0:25–0:55 | Explain fragmented data and the difference between a map image and a decision model. |
| 0:55–1:25 | Introduce Apex-Mars, Jezero and the source-backed prototype. |
| 1:25–2:15 | Show only working interactions: layers, provenance, base/stops, labelled estimates, experimental route and supported 3D. |
| 2:15–2:45 | Explain primary data, uncertainty, AI assistance and potential educational/research value. |
| 2:45–3:00 | Close with the next steps: validation, traceable brief and user evaluation. |

## Demonstration procedure

1. Verify the deployed or local version and supported terrain area.
2. Open Jezero and switch between imagery/topography.
3. Inspect one source card and its limitations.
4. Place a hypothetical base and science stops.
5. Explain straight-line estimates separately from experimental terrain routing.
6. Enter 3D only within supported coverage; explain decorative objects and illustrative meters.
7. Return to 2D and identify the incomplete roadmap items.

Do not demonstrate exportable brief, live conditions or validated science/return alternatives unless they have actually been implemented and checked.

## Video and slides

The original plan targets a 60–90 s video and a short slide deck covering problem, user, data, workflow, prototype, limits, team and next steps. Confirm actual official format requirements.

Record a reliable flow, use legible captions, retain credits and ensure English public materials. A recording is a fallback, not proof that the current deployment works.

## Submission text template

- **Name:** Apex-Mars.
- **Team:** Apex-Reapers.
- **Short description:** A browser-based Jezero exploration prototype connecting orbital imagery, scientific context and explicit planning assumptions.
- **NASA data use:** Identify exact CTX/HiRISE/Mars Trek products and distinguish integrated assets from research-only MEDA data.
- **Accomplishments:** Describe implemented behavior with evidence.
- **Limitations:** Partial coverage, assumed routing/resource parameters, incomplete validation and pending features.
- **Next steps:** Scientific/rights review, traceable brief and usability evaluation.
- **References:** Link the bibliography and original source pages.
- **AI disclosure:** Link the detailed usage statement; do not claim AI is a scientific authority.

## Q&A preparation

Be ready to explain why Jezero was selected; which missions contribute actual assets; how coordinates are handled; whether weather is live; whether routes are safe; how resources are estimated; why the routing grid is assumed; what 3D represents; how AI was used; and what remains unvalidated.

## Communication checklist

Use the current name while acknowledging historical Ares Atlas records. Prefer clear claims and readable evidence to exaggerated marketing. Verify every link, current deployment, external credit, model assumption and participant detail before submitting.
